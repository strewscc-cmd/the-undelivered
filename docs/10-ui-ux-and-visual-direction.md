# 10 — UI/UX and Visual Direction

## Visual identity

**Mood:** dark academia, literary mystery, old postal systems, archival paper, rain, smoke, and municipal records.

### Palette
- Parchment cream: primary document surfaces.
- Charcoal: background and text.
- Oxblood burgundy: emphasis and significant clues.
- Antique brass: details, stamps, and navigational accents.
- Muted slate/gray: secondary metadata and uncertain findings.

Avoid using red alone to imply guilt. Evidence status must also be conveyed through labels and text.

## Visual motifs

- A box of undelivered letters
- Postmarks and date stamps
- Folded correspondence and damaged paragraphs
- Old municipal maps and parcel boundaries
- Newspaper clippings
- Fire reports and typewritten administrative notes
- A station locker marked 07
- Archive shelves and inventory slips
- An evidence board showing people, land parcels, letters, and dates

## Interface guidelines

- Prioritize legibility over decorative distress effects.
- Documents must have a readable text version in addition to any scanned-paper presentation.
- Use zoom, highlight, and annotation interactions only when they help inspect a clue.
- Clearly distinguish original dates from discovery dates.
- Provide explicit evidence-status labels: Confirmed Fact, Supported Inference, Unresolved Question.
- Keep the inventory and evidence board revisitable.
- Do not force the player to memorize arbitrary numbers; all codes must be derived from available clues.

## Sound direction

- Rain and distant thunder
- Clock ticking
- Paper and envelope handling
- Drawers and archive boxes
- Soft room tone in the post office/municipal archive
- Carefully scripted voice recordings

Voice recordings should be added after the scripts are finalized. Every spoken clue must have a transcript/caption and must not be audio-only.

## Fictional artifacts

All newspaper clippings, government records, corporate materials, photographs, and letters should be clearly fictional assets for the game. Do not reuse real names or make a real company appear responsible for arson.
