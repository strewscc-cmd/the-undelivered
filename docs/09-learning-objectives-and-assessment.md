# 09 — Learning Objectives and Assessment

## Target learners

Advanced English learners at approximately CEFR B2.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the game, learners can:

1. Identify stated and implied information in personal letters and official documents.
2. Infer tone, purpose, and possible bias while citing textual evidence.
3. Compare information across sources and recognize inconsistencies in chronology and records.
4. Distinguish confirmed facts from supported inferences and unresolved questions.
5. Use expressions for giving directions in a meaningful navigation activity.
6. Express and justify opinions about the relevance and reliability of evidence.
7. Produce a coherent 100–150 word investigative report using relevant evidence, qualified claims, and clear language.

## English language objectives

- Reading for detail and inference
- Vocabulary in context
- Formal and informal register
- Pronoun reference and cohesion
- Paragraph coherence
- Chronology and sequencing
- Expressing opinions with reasons
- Hedging and qualifying claims
- Evidence-based writing

## Assessment structure

Two independent score tracks should be maintained.

### A. Investigation Score (100%)

- Puzzle accuracy: 40%
- Evidence connections: 35%
- Independent investigation / hint use: 25%

### B. English Proficiency Score (100%)

- Textual evidence: 30%
- Inference and reasoning: 30%
- Coherence and organization: 20%
- Language clarity: 20%

## Final Report task

Prompt:

“What is the most plausible explanation for the three fires, Thomas Wren's death, and Evelyn's disappearance? Use at least three pieces of evidence. Distinguish confirmed facts from inferences and identify at least one question that remains unresolved.”

Length: 100–150 words in English.

## Analytic writing rubric

### Evidence use — 30 points
- 27–30: Uses at least three relevant pieces of evidence and explains how they support the argument.
- 22–26: Uses relevant evidence, but one connection is weak or underexplained.
- 15–21: Includes some evidence but relies on summary or unsupported claims.
- 0–14: Gives few relevant details or offers assertions without evidence.

### Inference and reasoning — 30 points
- 27–30: Clearly distinguishes fact, inference, and uncertainty; considers limitations.
- 22–26: Reasoning is mostly sound, with minor overstatements.
- 15–21: Makes broad inferences or misses important limitations.
- 0–14: Treats unsupported theories as established facts.

### Coherence and organization — 20 points
- 18–20: Clear claim, logical sequence, cohesive links, appropriate conclusion.
- 14–17: Generally organized with minor gaps.
- 10–13: Some ideas are disconnected or repetitive.
- 0–9: Difficult to follow.

### Language clarity — 20 points
- 18–20: Clear B2-appropriate language with controlled grammar and useful hedging.
- 14–17: Meaning is clear despite minor errors.
- 10–13: Errors sometimes obscure relationships between ideas.
- 0–9: Language often prevents understanding.

## Fairness principles

- Accept different theories when supported by the available evidence.
- Do not grade a student's chosen theory solely for matching the teacher's interpretation.
- Reward acknowledging uncertainty.
- Keep investigation logic separate from English accuracy.
- Hints can affect independence scoring, but not the language score.
- Provide accessible transcripts for all audio.
