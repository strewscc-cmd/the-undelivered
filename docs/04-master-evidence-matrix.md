## Evidence classification rules

### Confirmed fact
Use when a proposition is directly supported by an authenticated source or by independent, consistent sources.

### Supported inference
Use when evidence makes a proposition plausible but does not eliminate alternative explanations.

### Unresolved question
Use when available evidence cannot distinguish between competing explanations.

### Character claim
A person may be mistaken, biased, deceptive, or incomplete. Their words are evidence that they made a statement, not automatically proof the statement is true.

## Canon-level strongest inferences

- Deliberate burning is the strongest overall theory, but not conclusively established.
- Evelyn may have been targeted to silence her investigation, but the perpetrator and her fate are unknown.
- Ashbourne benefited from land changes, but its involvement in arson is unproven.
- Postal interference is supported for identified letters, but this does not establish involvement in any fire.

## Authoring requirement

For each new evidence item, record: ID, source/creator, original date, discovery date, exact textual clue, supported conclusion, alternative explanation, and what it cannot prove.
