# 03 — Master Timeline

## Timeline rules

- Newspaper assets already created are the controlling source for their printed dates and reported event dates unless a later approved revision explicitly changes the assets.
- Distinguish the date a letter was written, mailed, processed, postmarked, received, and discovered.
- A processing date does not prove where the writer was on that date.
- The three fires occurred in nearby areas, not necessarily on the exact same parcel.
- Newspaper reporting is a source, not omniscient truth: preserve the difference between a reported fact, an official finding, and a reporter's or resident's inference.

### Newspaper 01 date lock

The first uploaded newspaper asset is dated **Tuesday, 20 October 1998** and says the warehouse fire occurred late Sunday evening. The implied event date is **Sunday, 18 October 1998**. Preserve those asset dates in all related records unless the image itself is revised.

This means Evelyn's investigation must already have begun before her father's death: the letters dated 8–17 October cannot all be presented as a response to a death that occurs on 18 October. After the fire, her inquiry can shift toward the circumstances of his death and the links between the land records and correspondence irregularities.

## Cross-document rules

1. Never use a 2026 discovery date as though it were the document's original date.
2. Do not give post-1998 knowledge to 1998 characters unless a source justifies it.
3. Do not let a character in 1998 predict the 2023 or 2026 fires.
4. Avoid claiming that all fires have one perpetrator.
5. The company's appearance in records across different dates is a lead, not a conviction.
6. Newspaper dates and explicit event dates in the three uploaded assets must match references in official reports.
7. Do not claim Evelyn began investigating only after Thomas's death; the dated letters establish that her inquiry was already underway before 18 Oct 1998.
