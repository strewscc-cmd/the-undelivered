## Rules for the final text

1. All personal letters and records must align with the master timeline and the newspaper dates.
2. Differentiate written, mailed, processed, postmarked, received, and discovered dates.
3. Each letter should have a distinct narrative or puzzle function.
4. Do not make any letter state the full solution directly.
5. A character must not know facts they could not reasonably know.
6. Avoid overly convenient phrasing that tells the player what the puzzle answer is.
7. Letters can be emotionally expressive; official records should be restrained and consistent.
8. F06 and F14 may be witness records rather than letters; their F IDs are retained for production continuity.
9. F07's intended recipient, F08/F09 recipients, F11 sender/recipient, F12 record type, and F15 archive path must be finalized before puzzle implementation.
10. A letter about Thomas's death must not use conclusively criminal language unless the speaker clearly marks it as belief or suspicion.

## Drafting workflow

Write each document in this format:
- Header: ID, title, writer, recipient, date written, date processed/discovered (where relevant).
- Full text.
- Intended clue.
- What it supports.
- What it does not prove.
- Cross-references.
- B2 language objectives.
