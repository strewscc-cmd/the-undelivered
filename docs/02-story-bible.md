# 02 — Story Bible / Master Canon

**Canon version:** 3.0  
**Status:** Core canon locked; individual document scripts still in development.

## Logline

In 2026, an investigative journalist reopens the story of Evelyn Wren, a journalism student who vanished in 1998 after investigating documents left by her father, Bellwether's mayor, who died in a suspicious fire. Three fires, a land-development company, discrepancies in official records, and undelivered letters appear connected—but the evidence never proves every suspected link.

## Official premise

In 1998, a fire in the Bellwether area killed Mayor Thomas Wren. Before his death, Thomas had opposed a land-development project after finding discrepancies in its documents. The circumstances of the fire were suspicious, but no definitive proof establishes arson or proves that Thomas was murdered.

Evelyn Wren, his 19-year-old daughter and a first-year journalism student, began investigating the documents left by her father. She discovered discrepancies in land records and irregularities in correspondence. She disappeared in October 1998 before her investigation reached a clear conclusion.

A second fire occurred in a nearby area in 2023. The case from 1998 was reviewed again because of similarities involving location and land records. A third fire in 2026 triggered a further review. During work on the old archives, a box of undelivered correspondence connected to Evelyn's investigation was discovered.

The player, an investigative journalist, attempts to connect the evidence.

## Immutable canon

1. The first fire took place in 1998 and killed Mayor Thomas Wren.
2. Thomas had opposed the land-development project after finding discrepancies in its documents.
3. There are indications that the 1998 fire may have been deliberate, but arson is not definitively proven.
4. No definitive evidence proves that Thomas's death was murder or that it was directly caused by a planned arson.
5. Evelyn was 19 in 1998, a first-year journalism student, and Thomas's daughter.
6. Evelyn investigated records related to her father's death and the land-development project.
7. Evelyn disappeared in October 1998. Her final fate is unknown.
8. Some of Evelyn's correspondence was withheld or not processed normally.
9. Fires occurred in nearby areas in 1998, 2023, and 2026. They are not necessarily on exactly the same parcel of land.
10. Ashbourne Land & Development appears in records related to the land and benefited from changes in land status or use.
11. Ashbourne's benefit does not prove that it caused or ordered any fire.
12. Evidence suggests Evelyn may have become a target of an effort to silence her investigation, but responsibility and her final fate remain unresolved.
13. Silas Reed's postal actions must be supported by postal records and the Retention Note. His knowledge of the fires is not established.
14. Dr Adrian Vale pressured Evelyn to stop investigating; this does not prove he was involved in any fire or in her disappearance.
15. No single character or document contains the entire answer.

## Final interpretive position

- **Strongest fire theory:** deliberate burning is the strongest theory based on patterns and inconsistencies, but not definitively established.
- **Company role:** Ashbourne benefited from changes to land use/status. The company's involvement in arson remains unproven.
- **Evelyn's fate:** she may have been targeted to silence her investigation, but whether she survived, left town, or became a victim is unknown.
- **Mayor's death:** suspicious circumstances merit further investigation; a murder conclusion is not proven.
- **Correspondence:** certain letters were held from normal processing. This shows interference with communication if the records are authentic, but does not itself identify the cause of the fires.

## Tone and themes

- Dark academia and archival mystery.
- Grief, unanswered questions, public records, institutional reputation, land interests, and the ethics of investigation.
- Tension should come from conflicting evidence and withheld information, not supernatural events.
- No supernatural explanation.
- Avoid presenting a real-world company or real public official as the fictional culprit; all institutions and characters are fictional.

## Writing rule: epistemic levels

Every statement in a puzzle or document must be classified during authoring as one of:
- **Confirmed fact**
- **Supported inference**
- **Unresolved question**
- **Character belief or speculation**

Do not allow a character's belief to silently become canon.
