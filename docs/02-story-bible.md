# 02 — Story Bible / Master Canon

**Canon version:** 3.1  
**Status:** Core canon aligned to newspaper asset dates; individual document scripts still in development.

## Logline

In 2026, an investigative journalist reopens the story of Evelyn Wren, a journalism student who vanished in 1998 while investigating disputed land records and correspondence irregularities. Her father, Bellwether's mayor, died in a suspicious fire during the same month. Three fires, a land-development company, discrepancies in official records, and undelivered letters appear connected—but the evidence never proves every suspected link.

## Official premise

Before October 1998, Mayor Thomas Wren had opposed a land-development project after finding discrepancies in its documents. His daughter Evelyn, 19, a first-year journalism student, had already begun examining Riverside land records and correspondence irregularities while her father was alive.

A warehouse fire near the Riverside Industrial Zone occurred late on Sunday, 18 October 1998, killing Thomas Wren. The Bellwether Chronicle reported his death in its Tuesday, 20 October 1998 edition. The cause of the fire remains unresolved; no definitive proof establishes arson or murder. After the fire, Evelyn's inquiry could also turn toward the circumstances of her father's death. She disappeared later in October 1998, before her investigation reached a clear conclusion.

A second fire damaged an unused storage building near the Riverside Industrial Zone on Saturday, 21 October 2023 at 8:00 PM, according to the newspaper asset dated Monday, 23 October 2023. The 1998 case was reviewed again because of similarities involving location and land records. A third fire occurred near the Riverside Industrial Zone in October 2026; the current newspaper asset does not specify an exact date. During work on old archives, a box of undelivered correspondence connected to Evelyn's investigation was discovered.

The player, an investigative journalist, attempts to evaluate the evidence.

## Immutable canon

1. The first fire occurred late on Sunday, 18 October 1998, and killed Mayor Thomas Wren. This date is inferred from the date and wording of Newspaper 01.
2. The Bellwether Chronicle published Newspaper 01 on Tuesday, 20 October 1998.
3. Thomas had opposed the land-development project after finding discrepancies in its documents.
4. There are indications that the 1998 fire may have been deliberate, but arson is not definitively proven.
5. No definitive evidence proves that Thomas's death was murder or that it was directly caused by a planned arson.
6. Evelyn was 19 in 1998, a first-year journalism student, and Thomas's daughter.
7. Evelyn's inquiry into land records and correspondence irregularities began before Thomas's death. After the fire, she could also investigate its circumstances.
8. Evelyn disappeared in October 1998. Her final fate is unknown.
9. Some of Evelyn's correspondence was withheld or not processed normally.
10. The second fire occurred on Saturday, 21 October 2023 at 8:00 PM, as stated in Newspaper 02, which was published Monday, 23 October 2023.
11. A third fire occurred near the Riverside Industrial Zone in October 2026. The exact event and publication dates remain unspecified by Newspaper 03.
12. The three fires occurred in nearby areas; they are not necessarily on exactly the same parcel of land.
13. Ashbourne Land & Development appears in records related to the land and benefited from changes in land status or use.
14. Ashbourne's benefit does not prove that it caused or ordered any fire.
15. Evidence suggests Evelyn may have become a target of an effort to silence her investigation, but responsibility and her final fate remain unresolved.
16. Silas Reed's postal actions must be supported by postal records and the Retention Note. His knowledge of the fires is not established.
17. Dr Adrian Vale pressured Evelyn to stop investigating; this does not prove he was involved in any fire or in her disappearance.
18. No single character or document contains the entire answer.

## Final interpretive position

- **Strongest fire theory:** deliberate burning is the strongest theory based on patterns and inconsistencies, but not definitively established.
- **Company role:** Ashbourne benefited from changes to land use/status. The company's involvement in arson remains unproven.
- **Evelyn's fate:** she may have been targeted to silence her investigation, but whether she survived, left town, or became a victim is unknown.
- **Mayor's death:** suspicious circumstances merit further investigation; a murder conclusion is not proven.
- **Correspondence:** certain letters were held from normal processing. This shows interference with communication if the records are authentic, but does not itself identify the cause of the fires.

## Tone and themes

- Dark academia and archival mystery.
- Grief, unanswered questions, public records, institutional reputation, land interests, and the ethics of investigation.
- Tension should come from conflicting evidence and withheld information, not supernatural events.
- No supernatural explanation.
- Avoid presenting a real-world company or real public official as the fictional culprit; all institutions and characters are fictional.

## Writing rule: epistemic levels

Every statement in a puzzle or document must be classified during authoring as one of:
- **Confirmed fact**
- **Supported inference**
- **Unresolved question**
- **Character belief or speculation**

Do not allow a character's belief to silently become canon.
